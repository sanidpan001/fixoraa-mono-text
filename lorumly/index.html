Lorem Ipsum Explained: What Placeholder Text Is, Where It Came From, and How to Generate It Well

A Lorumly field guide for designers, developers and writers

What is lorem ipsum, exactly?

Lorem ipsum is the world's most widely used placeholder text: a block of scrambled Latin-looking words that designers, developers and typesetters drop into a layout wherever the real words have not been written yet. Its job is deliberately boring. Because the text carries no meaning a reader can follow, nobody gets distracted by the content, and all attention stays where it belongs during the design stage — on the typography, the spacing, the hierarchy, the colours and the overall shape of the page. When a client reviews a mockup filled with lorem ipsum, they comment on the design. When the same mockup is filled with a half-written draft, they comment on the draft, and the design review quietly derails.

The passage is not random gibberish, which is a common misconception. It is derived from real Latin prose — sections 1.10.32 and 1.10.33 of Cicero's philosophical work De Finibus Bonorum et Malorum ("On the Ends of Good and Evil"), written in 45 BC. Over centuries of typesetting, the original sentences were shuffled, truncated and corrupted: words lost their endings, new fragments appeared, and the famous opening "Lorem ipsum dolor sit amet" was carved out of Cicero's line "dolorem ipsum" — "pain itself." The result reads like Latin, flows like Latin, but means nothing at all, which is precisely why it has survived for more than five hundred years of print and several decades of web design.

A short history of dummy text

Printers were using specimen text long before the web existed. In the 1500s, an unknown printer scrambled a galley of type to make a specimen book, and lorem ipsum entered the trade. It became an industry standard in the 1960s when Letraset released dry-transfer sheets of the passage for graphic designers, and it crossed into software in the 1980s when Aldus PageMaker shipped with lorem ipsum templates. Richard McClintock, a Latin professor at Hampden-Sydney College in Virginia, is credited with tracing the passage back to Cicero after noticing that "consectetur" — one of the passage's words — is rare enough in classical Latin literature to leave a searchable trail. Today the text ships inside design tools, code editors, theme templates and content management systems everywhere, and generators like Lorumly exist so you can produce exactly the amount you need, in the shape you need, without hunting for a block to copy.

Why placeholder text beats "text goes here"

It is tempting to fill a layout with repeated phrases like "Your headline here" or a row of x's, but those shortcuts distort the design. Real prose has a natural rhythm: words of varied length, sentences of varied shape, paragraphs that breathe. Lorem ipsum reproduces that rhythm faithfully because it is built from real language statistics. A headline repeated twenty times will never wrap the way a genuine sentence wraps, and a column of identical words hides problems — awkward line breaks, orphaned words, cramped line height — that only appear once real copy arrives. Good placeholder text is a stress test for typography. It reveals whether your font size is readable at arm's length, whether your measure (line length) is comfortable, and whether your spacing scale holds together across headings, body copy and captions.

There is also a workflow benefit. Placeholder text decouples design from copywriting, two jobs that rarely finish at the same speed. With a generator, a developer can build and test a template while the writer is still interviewing subject-matter experts; a designer can show a client three layout directions in an afternoon without waiting for approved text. The copy, when it lands, drops into a structure that has already been proven to work.

Paragraphs, sentences or words: choosing your measure

Lorumly lets you measure output three ways, and each suits a different job. Paragraphs are the workhorse: choose three to five and you can fill an article body, an about page or a product description area with realistic text blocks, complete with natural paragraph breaks. Paragraph mode is what you want when testing how a page scrolls, how headings interrupt body text, and how much vertical space a section really occupies. Sentences give finer control — ideal for card descriptions, testimonial placeholders, captions, error messages and list items, where one or two sentences must look right inside a tight component. The classic opening, and when to turn it off

The signature opening toggle controls whether your text begins with the famous first line. With it on, output starts "Lorem ipsum dolor sit amet, consectetur adipiscing elit…" — instantly recognisable to any client or stakeholder as placeholder text, which is often exactly the signal you want to send in an early review. There are good reasons to switch it off, though. If you are generating many blocks for the same page, repeating the identical opening in every section looks artificial and can mask repetition problems in the layout. Turning the opening off starts each generation mid-stream, the way real body copy usually reads. It is also the better choice for screenshot-based portfolio work, where a wall of identical openings can look careless even when the design is anything but.

Best practices for working with placeholder text

A few habits separate professional placeholder use from the pitfalls that give lorem ipsum a bad name. First, never ship it: placeholder text that reaches production embarrasses brands, confuses search engines and can even be indexed, so add a pre-launch check for the words "lorem ipsum" to every project checklist. Second, match volume to reality — generate roughly the amount of text the final copy is expected to fill, because a design tuned for three paragraphs will strain under twelve. Third, test extremes as well as averages: one very long block and one very short block will expose fragile layouts faster than ten medium ones. Fourth, remember accessibility: placeholder text should never be the only content a screen-reader user encounters in a prototype you are testing with real people; label mockups clearly. Finally, replace filler progressively. As real copy is approved section by section, swap it in and re-check the layout each time, rather than discovering at the deadline that the genuine headline is twice as long as the dummy one.
